HordeMagic.com Rules ​

Horde versions · Rules: published basic rules checked October 18, 2026

This is the site's EDH-style version, with a larger Horde, rarity-based waves, and decisions made in the Horde's interest.

Players, decks, and setup ​

Use one to four human players, each with an EDH deck, against a Horde library of approximately 300 cards. The Horde has no pilot and no hand. Prepare tokens and any scenario-specific counters.

Prepare the survivor decks and command zones and take normal opening hands and mulligans. Shuffle the Horde unless a specific scenario instructs otherwise. The survivors take three shared turns before the Horde's first turn.

Shared survivor turns ​

Survivors act together through untap, phases, combat, and end step. Their life total is shared. They still operate their own decks and cards.

Damage or life loss to a survivor reduces the shared total; life gain increases it. The Horde counts as a player.

The source distinguishes these wordings:

  • An effect referring to each player applies for each human player.
  • An effect referring to each opponent applies once for the survivor group.

When the Horde attacks, the survivor group is considered one player for that attack. The source explicitly allows the survivors' abilities that trigger when they are attacked to trigger.

Horde turn and waves ​

After setup, alternate survivor and Horde turns. By default, the Horde plays one wave per turn.

  1. Cast cards from the top of the Horde library, paying mana costs with its unlimited mana.
  2. Continue until an uncommon, rare, or mythic card is cast.
  3. That cast ends the wave. The current source uses casting, not entering the battlefield, as the stopping condition.
  4. Move to combat.
  5. During the post-combat main phase, use eligible activated abilities subject to the limits below.

Agree how the physical Horde list identifies a card's rarity; different printings can have different rarities.

Horde creatures and abilities ​

  • Horde creatures have haste and are goaded unless otherwise stated, such as by defender.
  • Only Horde creatures with defender may block.
  • The Horde has infinite mana for casting and activation costs.
  • The source applies summoning sickness to card-activated abilities and places their use in the post-combat main phase.
  • A permanent may have an activated ability activated only once per turn under this house rule.
  • The source calls for card-triggered abilities to happen immediately.

The source's broad wording about summoning sickness and “immediately” is not a full replacement for Magic's timing rules. If a chosen card requires a finer ruling about an untapped permanent, an ability without a tap cost, or a trigger on the stack, record that interpretation before using it.

Hand, draw, discard, and bounce ​

The Horde does not maintain a hand.

  • If it would draw a card, take that card and immediately cast it.
  • If a Horde-owned permanent would return to its hand, put it on top of the Horde library instead.
  • When offered a discard, the Horde may put the top card of its library into its graveyard.

Do not use Classic Horde's “recast from hand next main phase” rule in this version.

Damage, life gain, and choices ​

Each point of damage to the Horde mills a card from its library. The Horde has no ordinary life total and normally receives no benefit from life gain.

When the Horde must make a choice, choose in the Horde's best interest. This includes choosing targets. It is not the random-choice rule from Classic Horde.

Additional published interpretations ​

  • Exotic Orchard and Fellwar Stone: the source recommends letting these produce any color, even though the Horde has no lands.
  • Attack prevention: ignore static card rules that would prevent the Horde's creatures from attacking, including entering tapped or being unable to attack. Targeted effects, activated effects, and stun counters remain usable.
  • Monarch: the source assigns monarchy to the survivor group, but only one survivor draws its card. The group chooses the player who needs the draw.

These are this version's special interpretations, not universal Magic or Horde rules.

Victory and defeat ​

The survivors win only when both the Horde library and its battlefield are empty.

The Horde wins when the survivors run out of life, cannot draw, or reach the poison limit. Cards with alternate winning abilities, including “you win the game” effects, are not legal in this version.

The current source does not give a numerical poison threshold or fully specify whether a single survivor's empty library defeats the entire group. Choose those details before playing decks that can invoke them.

Optional modules ​

Choose modules before play and record the choice for a boxed set.

More waves ​

Increase the wave count for larger groups, or use the suggested repeating progression 1, 2, 3, 2, 1, 2, 3, 2, 1. A scenario using this should specify whether and how it repeats beyond the written sequence.

Legendary bosses ​

The source recommends roughly seven legendary creatures as bosses. With its optional damage-mill rule, when such a creature would go to the graveyard from damage milling, it enters the battlefield instead, triggers its enter-the-battlefield abilities, and then immediately phases out. It later phases in under normal phasing rules.

Horde life gain ​

Instead of ignoring Horde life gain, the group may put cards from its graveyard on the bottom of its library. The source does not specify the selection order or conversion rate, so a box adopting this module must provide those details.

Historical restrictions ​

The repository's older source notes preserve a ban list and two banned combinations. Those are retained as reference material; do not silently combine that older list with the current page's attack-prevention interpretation.
