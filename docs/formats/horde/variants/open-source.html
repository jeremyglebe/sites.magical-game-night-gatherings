Horde: Open Source Version

Horde versions · Rules: October 2011 follow-up to Classic Horde

This page includes the original play procedure together with the follow-up's setup adjustment, planeswalker procedure, and historical restrictions. These additions do not silently change the separate Classic rules page.

Players, decks, and setup

Use one to four survivors with Commander decks, or other agreed decks. Prepare a 100-card Horde with approximately 60 creature-token cards and 40 ordinary cards.

Shuffle the Horde, randomly set aside excess cards, and use this setup:

Survivors Shared life Horde cards Setup turns with the follow-up's adjustment
1 20 45 3
2 40 60 3
3 60 75 2
4 80 100 2

The Horde starts with no hand or permanents. Survivors prepare their decks and command zones, draw opening hands, and take normal mulligans. They take the listed number of shared turns before the Horde's first turn.

The reduction to two setup turns for three or four survivors is the author's proposed scaling adjustment. If the group declines it, use three and record that choice.

Survivor turns

Survivors share phases and steps, taking their draws and land plays individually and coordinating combat. They share life, but keep separate libraries, hands, permanents, and mana. Damage, life loss, and life gain change the shared total.

The Horde's turn

After the setup turns, alternate a Horde turn with a shared survivor turn.

  1. Reveal the top card of the Horde library.
  2. If it is a Zombie token, continue revealing.
  3. Stop at the first non-token card, or when the library runs out.
  4. Cast the revealed tokens, then cast the revealed non-token card last if there is one.
  5. Proceed to combat. Horde Zombies have haste and must attack if able.

The Horde's spells use the stack and players can respond, including countering them. Having infinite mana does not mean they bypass the stack.

The Horde attacks the survivor team. Survivors can block together for the team. Horde Zombies cannot block, so attacking the Horde does not require getting past its Zombies.

Horde mana, choices, and cards

The Horde has unlimited mana for costs. Mana taxes such as Propaganda or Mana Leak do not stop it by requiring a payment it cannot afford. Non-mana costs still require whatever the card instructs.

Build the Horde to minimize choices. When an effect nevertheless requires a choice, make it as randomly as possible among legal options.

A permanent returned to the Horde's hand is cast again during its next main phase. The Horde starts with no hand; that does not erase the original rule for cards returned to its hand.

When a card interaction does not function in this automated format, the original source asks the group to choose a fair handling or cycle the problematic card. Agree the exact replacement procedure if using that escape hatch; the source does not define its cycling cost or timing.

Attacking planeswalkers

At the beginning of Horde combat, if survivors control any planeswalkers, flip a coin.

  • Heads: randomly choose an order for those planeswalkers. For each one in that order, randomly allocate one available Zombie attacker per point of that planeswalker's loyalty. Stop allocating when no eligible attackers remain. Any attackers not allocated to a planeswalker attack the survivor team.
  • Tails: use the normal attack against the survivors.

This allocates a number of Zombies, not damage equal to loyalty. Survivors may defend their planeswalkers using normal blocking rules. The source presents this as its answer to the original rules' missing planeswalker handling.

Damage and victory

The Horde has no life total. Each point of damage it receives mills one card. Survivors may attack the Horde; its Zombies cannot block.

The survivors win when the Horde library is empty and its remaining Zombies are gone. The Horde wins when shared survivor life reaches zero or less.

Survivor ban list from the follow-up

The source's October 25, 2011 list prohibits:

Aether Flash; Aurification; Barbed Foliage; Caltrops; Crawlspace; Dueling Grounds; Dread; Elesh Norn, Grand Cenobite; Ensnaring Bridge; Eradicate; Grindstone; Haunting Echoes; Island Sanctuary; Lethal Vapors; Leyline of Singularity; Magus of the Moat; Mind Funeral; Moat; No Mercy; Platinum Angel; Platinum Emperion; Silent Arbiter; Solitary Confinement; Stormtide Leviathan; Surgical Extraction; Tainted Aether; Teferi's Moat; Time Stretch; Trepanation Blade; Urabrask the Hidden.

This is the historical list for this version, not a claim that all Horde groups use it. Apply the underlying deck format's legality as well.

The previously archived note listed Arteeoh, Dread Scavenger in this list; the original article actually names Dread. The list above follows the original article.

Optional deck changes

The source recommends changing the enemy deck's cards to adjust difficulty, including stronger creatures and sweepers. Its Sliver, Cat, and Merfolk lists are alternate deck ideas rather than automatic rule changes. Fixed survivor decks can be balanced as a boxed set.

The original game's unspecified edge cases remain unspecified unless this page supplies a rule; changing the enemy deck does not resolve poison, alternate wins, or all unusual card interactions.

Sources: original Horde rules and Horde Magic: An Open Source Project, Peter Knudson. Archived follow-up notes.
