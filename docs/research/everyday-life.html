Everyday Life and Ordinary New York ​

Research date: October 19, 2026. A card catalogue for making mundane life the main attraction: residents, pigeons, pets, lunch, newspapers, rent, homework, shops, tools, and public transportation. Marvel and Spider-Man are the starting point; other Magic settings and crossovers supply additional candidates.

The theme is what a card represents. An ordinary person's game ability can draw cards or grant counters without making that person a superhero. Likewise, School Daze qualifies through its homework premise even though it also has a Fight Crime mode. The actual rules of the card still apply. No card text, creature type, or format restriction is changed by appearing in this research.

This snapshot contains 524 individually catalogued cards: 97 Marvel entries and 427 additional candidates. The fit labels comprise 125 Direct, 299 Setting, and 100 Almost entries. Ordinary-animal token examples are listed separately.

How the fit labels work ​

LabelMeaning
DirectA strong ordinary-life premise that can fit the city joke: a person at work, a pet, a meal, a real object, or an everyday activity. A superhero can appear in the surrounding story without disqualifying the ordinary subject.
SettingThe person, animal, object, or activity is ordinary, but the era, habitat, species' real-world range, artwork, or franchise is less suited to present-day New York. A bear belongs here because it is ordinary wildlife, not normal subway wildlife.
AlmostSomething specifically extraordinary is part of the subject: powers, a transformation, an alien infection, an animated object, an anthropomorphic fantasy species, or special joke-card rules. Every such row explains the exception.

These are editorial judgments, not official Scryfall categories or legality labels. Some borderline choices will reasonably move after looking at the cards together. Venomized Cat is deliberately included: the joke of a little cat that caught alien goo survives, while its Symbiote Cat Villain identity remains explicit.

Particularly useful starting points ​

For the ordinary New York idea, begin with City Pigeon, Aunt May, J. Jonah Jameson, Gallant Citizen, Selfless Police Captain, Daily Bugle Reporters, Supportive Parents, Common Crook, Taxi Driver, and Willie Lumpkin, Postman. The noncreature side is just as central: Bagel and Schmear, Hot Dog Cart, Daily Bugle Newspaper, Peter Parker's Camera, News Helicopter, Subway Train, Passenger Ferry, Rent Is Due, School Daze, and Urban Retreat.

Outside Marvel, Courier of Comestibles, Action News Crew, Skateboard, Manhole Cover, Big Apple, 3 a.m., and the TMNT pizza cards bring useful modern-city material. Dog Walker, Citizen's Crowbar, Jackhammer, Tramway Station, and Getaway Car offer strong ordinary roles and objects from fantasy cities. Sanctuary Cat, Helpful Hunter, Warehouse Tabby, Selfless Savior, Carrier Pigeons, Sewer Rats, and Brood of Cockroaches make the animal side substantial enough to explore without turning it into a collection of superheroes.

The names above are entry points, not a proposed decklist. The linked category pages preserve the much larger card-by-card catalogue.

Rules distinctions that matter when building ​

News Helicopter is an artifact creature — Construct. It is not a Vehicle, despite depicting a helicopter. Subway Train and Passenger Ferry are Vehicles and are not creature cards in the deck. J. Jonah Jameson and Aunt May are Human Citizens, Selfless Police Captain is a Human Detective, and City Pigeon is a Bird. A shared city theme does not make them share a creature type.

For Creature Feature, count the actual types and the complete deck-construction requirements. Human could connect many of the people, with birds or other non-Humans using the permitted support space; Citizen is a narrower choice. This reference does not authorize treating all residents as Citizens or all animals as one type.

Keep full modal cards and both faces where applicable. Including School Daze for Do Homework does not remove Fight Crime. A box that changes a rule must deliberately document the change against its chosen base format. The same care applies to Horde targeting, optional abilities, repeat activations, and effects that require a player to make decisions.

The project's usual preference remains distinct nonbasic cards. Some candidates explicitly reward repeated names—such as Gathering Throng, Squadron Hawk, Pack Mastiff, and Relentless Rats. Their notes make that visible; inclusion in a catalogue does not commit a box to duplicates. Basic lands and token quantities follow the selected game's rules and actual component needs.

Coverage and research method ​

This is a best-effort discovery catalogue, not a claim to have found every possible mundane image ever printed. There is no reliable single query for this theme. Names alone miss ordinary scenes on abstractly named spells, while a Human or Cat type alone does not prove that a character is unpowered or an animal is mundane.

The search combined:

  1. A broad pass through the released Marvel-art card pool, including SPM, SPE, MSH, MSC, MAR, and Marvel Secret Lair material. See the separate Marvel set reference for the complete code inventory.
  2. The TMNT card pools and targeted passes through New Capenna, Karlov Manor, Neon Dynasty, Fallout, and Doctor Who for city life, civilians, and contemporary objects.
  3. Cross-set creature searches for Citizens, ordinary pets, birds, rats, squirrels, bears, other animals, and common insect/pest names. Obvious anthropomorphic heroes, spirits, and monsters were excluded or moved to Almost.
  4. Food and Vehicle searches, plus grouped name searches for occupations, household tools, transport, places, money problems, meals, school, leisure, maintenance, and crime. This explicitly included noncreature cards.
  5. Exact-name checks, Oracle-type and rules-text review, and selected artwork checks for ambiguous candidates. Each row links a specific printing so that visual fit can be reviewed before selecting the physical cards. The catalogue does not claim that every alternate artwork was inspected.

Card facts were retrieved through the project's Scryfall MCP server. Searches used a release cutoff of date<=2026-10-19; token and art-card records were separated from the ordinary-card list. Large result sets were divided into smaller queries rather than treating the first result page as the entire pool.

The saved search log records the successful search families and their scopes. It is a reproducibility aid, not a second uncurated recommendation list. Search results overlap, and a record count is not a count of separate recommended cards.

A named character's civilian job, an alternate artwork, or an ordinary-sounding title can be worth considering without silently erasing the extraordinary part.
