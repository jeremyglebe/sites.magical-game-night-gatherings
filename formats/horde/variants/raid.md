# Horde Raid

[Horde versions](../) · **Draft scenario framework: Peter Knudson's 2013 Raid variant**

Raid uses the original Horde game with an ordered enemy deck, artifact loot, and a prohibition on survivor attacks against the Horde. The original article calls it a work in progress. Its actual procedures are below, followed by the scenario decisions the article leaves open.

## Players and materials

Use one to four survivors with Commander decks or other agreed survivor decks. Prepare an enemy deck divided into named quest or stage packets, any boss cards, artifact loot, and the required tokens and counters.

Choose and record the scenario before play. Do not randomly cut a staged deck down using a Classic Horde player-count table without specifying how the cut preserves required stages and bosses.

## Setup

1. Put the scenario's stage packets in their required order.
2. Randomize the cards within each stage, while preserving any specifically positioned boss cards.
3. Stack the stages so the first stage is drawn first. Do not shuffle the combined library.
4. Prepare survivor libraries and commanders, draw opening hands, and take normal mulligans.
5. Unless the scenario changes the original Horde setup, use **20 shared life per survivor** and **three shared setup turns**.
6. The Horde begins with no hand or permanents. After the setup turns, alternate Horde and survivor turns.

## Survivor turns and combat

Survivors take their turns together, with individual libraries, hands, mana, and land plays. They share life and defend together against Horde attacks.

**Survivors cannot attack the Horde.** This is the Raid replacement for Classic Horde's direct offensive combat. The article does not automatically prohibit every noncombat damage or mill effect; a scenario that wants that additional restriction must state it.

## Revealing a wave and finding loot

1. Reveal the next card from the staged Horde library.
2. If it is a creature token, add it to the wave and keep revealing.
3. If it is an artifact, treat it as survivor loot. It does **not** count as the non-token card that ends the wave, so continue revealing.
4. Stop on the first other non-token card, or when the library runs out.
5. Cast the revealed enemy tokens and then the wave's non-token spell, using the normal Horde stack procedure.
6. Give artifact loot to the survivors using the scenario's declared allocation and timing rule.

The article identifies artifacts as loot but does not specify who receives them, whether they enter immediately or go to a hand, or how equipment is assigned. Those details must be supplied by a completed Raid box.

## Horde combat and effects

Horde creatures have haste and attack the survivors when able. Survivors can block together. Use the original Horde's unlimited mana and random choices among legal options.

Horde spells and abilities can be responded to. A card returned to the Horde's hand can be cast on its next main phase under the original rule. The scenario must state any replacement for these procedures.

Noncombat damage to the Horde still mills one card per point under the inherited rules unless the scenario explicitly changes it. The ban on attacking does not itself define how a milled boss should be handled.

## Stages, victory, and defeat

The stage order creates the sequence of encounters. The source does not add an automatic battlefield reset or heal between stages. If a scenario uses either, its rules must give the exact trigger and effect.

The inherited Horde objective is to exhaust the enemy deck and defeat its remaining creatures while preserving the survivors' shared life. The Horde wins when shared survivor life reaches zero or less. A scenario may replace this with a boss objective or another loss condition, but must write it out.

## Published example: The Dragonborn

The article uses this ordered scenario:

1. Green cards and tokens.
2. First dragon boss.
3. White cards and tokens.
4. Second dragon boss.
5. Black cards and tokens.
6. Red cards and Zombie tokens.
7. Final boss.

Randomize within the listed groups. The author suggests a stage reset or additional sweepers as possible future tuning, not as finalized rules.

## Published Rat Horde scenario additions

The same article gives another Horde deck example with two specific additions. They are not universal Raid rules:

- When a poison-counter card is revealed, add it to the scenario's poison total. At **ten** such counters, the survivors lose.
- Treat a Sorin emblem card as a token flip: it continues the reveal sequence and provides the emblem's continuing creature bonus.

Use the example's actual cards and a declared scenario setup if adopting those modules.

## Decisions required for a complete Raid box

- Exact enemy, boss, and survivor decklists and supported player counts.
- Loot ownership, placement, allocation, and timing.
- Whether a wave may reveal through a stage boundary and how stage transitions are announced.
- Handling of bosses that are milled, exiled, or otherwise skipped.
- Any between-stage reset or recovery procedure.
- Exact scenario victory conditions and additional failure conditions.
