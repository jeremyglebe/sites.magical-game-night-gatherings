# Horde: Archived Wiki Version

[Horde versions](../) · **Rules: wiki snapshot preserved in this repository**

This page gives the procedures recorded in the repository's wiki notes. It is a distinct source version, not a claim that every Horde group uses these rules or that the snapshot matches the current wiki.

## Players and materials

Use one to four survivors and an automated Horde. Survivors bring their own decks, normally Commander decks. A typical Horde has 100 cards: approximately 60 creature-token cards and 40 ordinary cards, selected to avoid decisions and suit the Horde's theme.

Prepare the tokens, counters, and shared life tracker needed by those decks.

## Setup

1. Shuffle survivor decks and the Horde. Prepare commanders where applicable.
2. Survivors draw opening hands and take normal mulligans.
3. Start the survivor team at **20 life per player**, combined into one shared total.
4. Survivors take **three shared setup turns** before the Horde's first turn.
5. After setup, alternate survivor and Horde turns.

The snapshot describes random removal of Horde cards as a difficulty option, but does not supply the original article's exact player-count table. If using that option, record how many cards are set aside.

## Survivor turns

Survivors take their phases and steps simultaneously, similar to shared-team-turn Magic. They coordinate attacks and blocks as a team. Each player uses their own cards and resources; damage and life changes affect the shared total.

## Horde turns

1. At the start of the Horde's turn, reveal cards until reaching a non-token card or exhausting the library.
2. Cast the revealed token cards first, then the non-token card if there is one.
3. The Horde also casts cards from its hand or graveyard when applicable under their instructions.
4. Proceed to combat. Horde creatures have haste and must attack if able.

The Horde has unlimited mana. Make any required choices as randomly as possible among legal options. Its spells and abilities still interact with the normal Magic stack.

The archived snapshot does not separately state a universal blocking restriction; do not silently import HordeMagic.com's defender-only restriction into it.

## Damage, life loss, and life gain

The Horde has no life total. Damage **or life loss** that would affect it mills that many cards from its library.

Lifelink works for survivor creatures: damage can both mill the Horde and gain survivor life. Horde life gain does not return milled cards to its library in this recorded version.

## Winning and losing

Survivors win when all three conditions hold:

- The Horde library is empty.
- The Horde hand is empty.
- The Horde controls no creatures.

This differs from the HordeMagic.com requirement to clear its entire battlefield. A noncreature Horde permanent does not, by itself, prevent this snapshot's victory condition.

The Horde wins when shared survivor life reaches zero or less.

## Optional difficulty changes

The snapshot proposes the following adjustable rules. Choose and state their values before play:

- Change the number of survivor setup turns.
- Change each survivor's starting-life contribution.
- Randomly remove a specified portion of the Horde before play.
- Change the Horde's card list or use a defined sideboard.
- Replace random Horde decisions with a survivor-consensus decision procedure.
- Let Horde token cards remain in the graveyard instead of ceasing to exist.
- Permit or prohibit attacks during survivor setup turns.

Changing token-to-spell ratios is also a deck-design adjustment. A boxed set must state the actual chosen settings rather than merely saying its difficulty is adjusted.
