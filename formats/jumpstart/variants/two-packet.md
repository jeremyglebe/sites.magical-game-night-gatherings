# Two-Packet Jumpstart

[Jumpstart parent page](../) · **Rules: two prepared packets per player, using ordinary two-player Magic**

## Players and materials

This ruleset is for **two players**. Each player needs **two Jumpstart packets**, for four packets total. Each packet contains **twenty playable cards, including its lands**. Theme cards, packet dividers, tokens, and ordinary reminder inserts are kept separately and do not count toward those twenty cards.

Supply life counters and any tokens, counters, or other components required by the cards. Use official packets or a prepared custom box with complete twenty-card packet lists. A custom packet is built before the play session using the [packet-building guide](../building-packets.md).

Jumpstart's deck-assembly procedure comes from the official product. The opening-hand and game procedures below use ordinary two-player Magic. If an event or box specifies a different procedure, such as a free first mulligan, that is an explicit addition to this version.

## Select packets

1. Identify the available packet pool and read any box-specific pairing restrictions before distributing packets.
2. Give each player two complete packets. With unopened retail boosters, each player opens their two boosters. With a reusable box, use the box's stated selection method. If it has none, shuffle its packet-divider cards face down and deal two to each player, then take the matching packets.
3. Set aside the theme cards, dividers, tokens, and other non-deck inserts.
4. Each player may inspect their own two packets before combining them. A known packet list does not reveal the order of the shuffled deck.

The face-down divider deal is this catalogue's default for distributing a reusable collection; buying random boosters is not required. A box may instead allow players to choose themes or draft whole packets, but it must supply that selection procedure. The unselected packets remain outside the game.

## Assemble the decks

1. Combine **all twenty cards from each selected packet** into a single **forty-card deck**.
2. Keep the lands that came with both packets. There is no separate land-adding or card-cutting step.
3. Keep each player's deck, library, hand, graveyard, and other zones separate from their opponent's.

The combined deck is not a pool from which to choose a smaller deck. Do not replace cards from unused packets or a personal collection during the game. This setup supplies no sideboard or extra companion card.

Two packets of the same color or theme can be combined. Copies of a card supplied by the two packets remain in the deck; Jumpstart does not impose singleton or the constructed four-copy restriction on the contents of those packets. This catalogue's preference to design custom packets with few duplicates is a preparation choice, not a rule that removes cards from official packets.

Cards from different Jumpstart releases can be combined. Custom boxes can define their own card pools, but should state any restrictions before players select packets. A card's legality in Standard or Commander is not an additional restriction on this Jumpstart game.

## Set up the game

1. Randomly determine who chooses which player takes the first turn, using a die roll, coin flip, or another agreed method.
2. Each player starts at **20 life**.
3. Each player thoroughly shuffles their combined deck and offers it to their opponent to cut or shuffle. It becomes their library.
4. Each player draws **seven cards**.
5. Resolve mulligans as described below.
6. Resolve any actions a card permits from an opening hand, in starting-player order, then begin the first turn.

### Mulligans

Use the normal London mulligan for a two-player game:

1. Starting with the player taking the first turn, each player declares whether to keep their hand or take a mulligan.
2. Each player taking a mulligan shuffles their hand back into their library and draws seven cards again.
3. That player puts a number of cards equal to their total number of mulligans on the bottom of their library in any order: one after their first mulligan, two after their second, and so on.
4. Repeat for players who still want to mulligan. A player who keeps their hand takes no further mulligans. Stop when everyone has kept, or a player's opening hand would be zero cards.

This version has **no free first mulligan**. A mulligan changes the opening hand, not the selected packets or deck contents.

## Turns, spells, and combat

The starting player **skips the draw step of their first turn**. After that exception, players alternate ordinary Magic turns, with the normal beginning phase, first main phase, combat phase, second main phase, and ending phase.

Use normal land-play limits, mana costs, spell timing, priority, combat, and card text. Jumpstart supplies no extra mana, shared resources, free spells, or special combat procedure. The [shared rules reference](../../../rules/) explains the underlying Magic rules assumed by the catalogue.

Legendary creatures are shuffled into the deck like other cards. They do not become commanders. There is no commander selection, command-zone casting, commander tax, commander damage, or Commander color-identity restriction. Ordinary rules such as the legend rule still apply.

Packet labels and sleeve stickers are sorting aids. They do not change a card's name, color, types, abilities, or ownership, and they do not authorize looking at a hidden card. A thematic character or team label does not grant an ability that is absent from the actual card.

## Winning and losing

Normal Magic victory and loss conditions apply. A player normally loses when they have **zero or less life**, have **ten or more poison counters**, attempt to draw more cards than remain in their library, or are made to lose by a card effect. They may also concede. Merely having an empty library does not cause a loss until a required draw cannot be completed.

When one player loses, the other wins. Card effects can provide other ways to win or modify a loss condition. If both players lose simultaneously, the game is a draw. Jumpstart adds no separate scoring, boss objective, or alternate victory condition.

## Another game

The procedure above plays a single game. Players may shuffle the same pair of packets for a rematch or reset the packets and select new combinations.

If playing a match with the same decks, agree on its length before starting. The loser of the previous game chooses who takes the first turn in the next game. After a drawn game, the player who made that choice previously makes it again. Keep the selected forty-card decks intact between games unless the box explicitly defines a different match structure.

## Reset a reusable box

1. Collect cards from every zone, including cards that changed controllers, were exiled, or were set aside to represent another card. Restore double-faced cards to their front faces for sorting.
2. Remove tokens, counters, and other non-deck components.
3. Sort the cards face up by their packet markers, including the basic lands. Each physical packet has its own identifier.
4. Count **twenty cards per packet**, then return each packet to its matching divider or container.
5. If a count is wrong or a marker is missing, check that packet's stored list before returning it to the available pool.

The [preparation guide](../building-packets.md#double-sleeve-and-mark-every-card) explains how to protect the markers inside sleeves. Its decklists are a reference for missing cards and changes; ordinary cleanup uses the markers and a card count.
