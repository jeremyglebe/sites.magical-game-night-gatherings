# Building and Labeling Custom Jumpstart Packets

[Jumpstart parent page](./) · [Full play rules](variants/two-packet.md) · **Preparation guide: this catalogue's reusable custom boxes**

Build the packets before game night so both setup and cleanup are quick. Each packet is a reusable twenty-card half-deck. Players combine two, play using the [two-packet rules](variants/two-packet.md), then sort them back into their original packets.

The building preferences and sleeve method here are local conventions for our custom boxes. They do not change the underlying Magic rules or require retail Jumpstart packets to use the same card-selection constraints.

## Choose a theme and a game plan

Give each packet a recognizable theme and a short description of how it plays. A character group, creature type, mechanical theme, or setting can provide the identity; the selected cards still need to work together on the table.

Choose the box's power level, intended players, and card pool before assembling packets. Cards can come from different sets and compatible settings. Ordinary card rules still apply: a robot-looking Construct remains a Construct, for example, and only receives a Robot-specific benefit if its actual types or a card effect qualify it.

The base format remains the starting point. Add a box-specific rule only when it serves a clear purpose, and write its procedure and scope on the box's page. A theme by itself does not create a rules exception.

## Build exactly twenty cards, including lands

1. Choose the packet's colors. A single-color packet is an accessible starting point because another packet can supply a second color.
2. Start with a proposed balance of **twelve nonland cards and eight lands**, then adjust the contents after playing. This is a design starting point, not a universal Jumpstart requirement; the packet must still total twenty cards.
3. Include enough early plays, ways to interact, and ways to finish a game. Spread the mana costs so the packet can contribute before its most expensive card becomes castable.
4. Supply lands that cast this packet's spells while leaving room for the other packet to function. Check demanding colored costs and include suitable fixing where needed.
5. Keep synergy useful with a variety of partners. A packet should have a functioning plan without requiring one exact partner or one card to appear in every opening hand.
6. Record every card and quantity, including lands. Store tokens, counters, theme cards, and explanatory aids separately from the twenty deck cards.

Multicolor packets need enough mana support to work with their intended partners. If a packet only works with specific partners, list those combinations on its divider and on the box page before players choose. Do not promise unrestricted mixing for a box that has not been built for it.

The designer supplies complete packets; players do not add lands or repair their decks between selecting packets and starting a game.

## Prefer variety; explain duplicates

Our default is **one copy of each nonbasic card across packets that can be combined**. Basic lands can repeat. Use different cards that perform related jobs to support a theme while keeping the individual cards varied.

A duplicate can be justified by a specific mechanical need, such as an essential engine piece or a strategy that depends on repeated copies of a particular card. Record the reason in the packet's design notes. A card being the most efficient option, or having many spare copies available, is not by itself the reason to include it repeatedly.

This is a custom-box design preference. Official packets can contain duplicates, and combining those packets does not require removing them. An exception in a custom box should be deliberate and recorded rather than silently changing the preference.

## Give every physical packet a distinct identity

Prepare one identifier per packet, not just one identifier for the whole box. Two Spider-themed packets need different identifiers so they can be separated after being shuffled together.

A small thematic image can identify a packet. For example, a Spider-Verse packet could use a distinctive Spider-themed face or emblem. Add a short code or number when several packets use related artwork: **MV-03** is a sample packet code, not a Magic set code. Keep identifiers unique across any boxes intended to be mixed.

Put the same identifier on:

- Every one of the packet's twenty cards, using the sleeve method below.
- Its divider, storage pocket, or container.
- Its complete packet list.

The divider should also show the packet's name, colors, game plan, and any pairing restrictions. Keep the card list available for replacing a missing card or updating the packet, rather than requiring players to compare every card against it during routine cleanup.

## Materials

- The complete twenty-card packet, including lands.
- Twenty clear inner sleeves that fit inside the chosen outer sleeves.
- Twenty opaque-backed outer sleeves, identical to those on every packet that can be combined.
- Twenty small stickers bearing the packet's identifier, plus a matching marker for its divider or container.
- The packet list, storage divider or container, and required tokens or counters.

The stickers can be thematic rather than transparent. Choose small, flat labels that fit in a front corner without hiding information needed to play. Avoid raised or bulky decorations that distort the sleeve. The outer sleeve will protect the sticker's edge during shuffling, so the sticker does not have to withstand direct contact with other cards on its own.

## Double-sleeve and mark every card

1. Put each card into a clear inner sleeve.
2. Apply its packet sticker to the **outside front surface of that inner sleeve**, near the bottom-left corner. The adhesive goes on the sleeve, **never on the card**.
3. Keep the marker clear of the card's name, mana cost, rules text, and power/toughness or loyalty information. Adjust its size and placement if the card's layout needs it.
4. Put the sleeved card into an **opaque-backed outer sleeve**. The marker is visible through the clear front and protected between the two sleeves, where it cannot catch directly on another card while shuffling.
5. Repeat for **all twenty cards, including basic lands**. Use a consistent marker size and position throughout the box, and keep the backs and edges indistinguishable across packets.
6. Mark the packet divider or container with the same identifier and confirm the completed packet contains twenty cards.

Identical outer sleeves preserve hidden information when two packets are shuffled together. Different sleeve backs would reveal packet membership in a library or an opponent's hand, so they are not this guide's default cleanup method. A box adopting that different information rule must say so explicitly.

These are permanent packet-identification labels, not Magic's in-game sticker mechanic. They do not add abilities or change a card's characteristics.

Try the method on two packets first. Check that the combined deck shuffles comfortably, the labels remain readable, card information is visible, and the sleeves sit flat before preparing the rest of the box.

## Playtest the combinations and the cleanup

Test each packet with several intended partners, including same-color and different-color combinations where those are supported. Check whether players can cast their spells, participate early, interact with each other, and finish games. Also check whether one packet or combination consistently overwhelms the rest.

Keep adjustments within ordinary card and format rules whenever possible. Change the card selection, mana support, or pairing restrictions when those solve the issue. Record a new box-specific rule only if it is actually needed, with enough detail that another group can apply it without guessing.

Test putting the packets away as part of the same session. The intended reset is:

1. Recover all deck cards from every zone and return any borrowed or controlled cards to their physical packets. Restore double-faced cards to their front faces.
2. Set aside tokens and other play aids.
3. Sort the cards into piles by their visible packet markers.
4. Count twenty cards in each pile, including lands.
5. Put each pile behind its matching divider or into its matching container.

Use the saved list to resolve missing cards, an incorrect count, or a damaged marker. Repair those issues before the packet goes back into the selection pool. When replacing a card or sleeve, restore its packet marker and update the list if the contents changed.

## What the boxed-set page records

A finished Jumpstart box should list:

- The [two-packet play rules](variants/two-packet.md) as its base, or a different complete ruleset if it changes that procedure.
- Its supported player count, packet inventory, and each packet's identifier and twenty-card list. Supply at least two physical packets per simultaneous player.
- How players select packets and which combinations are supported.
- Any deliberate duplicate cards and their design reasons.
- Every additional or replacement game rule, including its timing and exact effect.
- Required tokens, counters, dividers, and the marking and reset instructions.

The goal is a complete box that can be taken off the shelf, played, and put away again. Buying new cards or following later set releases is not part of its play procedure.
